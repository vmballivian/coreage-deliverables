6. Iteration gameplan

The 4 weeks exist to identify which axes carry the conversion, then concentrate budget on them.

What I'm testing

Four dimensions vary across the 10 launch creatives:

  • Which symptom converts. Sulphur burps, constipation, muscle loss, GLP-1 fog, rebound hunger, plateau. I don't know which pain is loudest for this audience. The cheapest-CPA symptom tells me what to lean into.
  • v1 (fear) vs v2 (curiosity). Two emotional leads on the lander, same product, same checkout. Same audience. The winner tells me the framing that closes solution-aware GLP-1 users in 2026.
  • Format: static vs AI UGC video vs cartoon. The audience filters polished agency creative as ads. I don't yet know whether they trust UGC, low-fi cartoon, or both equally. Mix in market lets me see.
  • Mechanic: gesture, ingestible ritual, or authority dismissal. All three are validated in adjacent DR funnels. None has been validated specifically on GLP-1 patients buying a probiotic. The first week is the read.

How I read signal

Three things tell me a creative is working: people clicking it (hook landed), people converting after clicking (offer fit), and the unit economics being defensible (ROAS supports continued spend). A creative can pass one and fail the others. CTR strong but CVR dead means the ad is selling something the lander doesn't deliver. CVR strong but CTR low means the hook is too narrow.

What I do with the signal

  • Losers go fast. A creative that is not pulling clicks does not get a sympathy week. The audience pool is finite and impressions on a dead creative raise frequency on the rest. I pull early and free the budget for the next iteration.
  • Winners get cloned with one variable changed. When something wins, my next move is not "spend more on it" first. It is "spend more on it AND produce 2 variations that change ONE thing" (the visual, the headline lead, the CTA copy). That tells me whether the win was the symptom, the framing, or just the creative.
  • Symptom wins compound. If sulphur burps win across two different creative formats, I produce more ads on that symptom (different formats again) before producing ads on a new symptom. The audience is telling me where the loud pain is.
  • Winners get scaled gradually, not doubled. Slow daily increases on a winning creative, not big jumps.
  • LP losers pause, do not die. If v1 or v2 clearly underperforms with enough traffic to call it, I pause it (not delete) and reroute traffic to the winner. The losing variant becomes the seed for v3 once I know why it lost.

Failure modes I want to avoid

  • Keeping creatives alive on sentiment. If I like an ad and it is not working, I still kill it.
  • Scaling a winner before iterating. A single winner with no variations is fragile. The first 3 days of a winner go to producing iterations, not just raising budget on the original.
  • Treating week 1 numbers as gospel. Week 1 is exploration. Statistical confidence is low. I make directional calls, not final ones.
  • Meta policy strikes. Repeated rejections on the advertorial framing accumulate against the ad account. Two strikes on the same creative = replace, not appeal. I have buffers planned (subdomain, fallback direct-to-product LPs).

Concrete numerical thresholds (CTR cutoffs, CPA limits, frequency caps, scale increments) I'll set at the end of week 1, anchored to our actual numbers.
